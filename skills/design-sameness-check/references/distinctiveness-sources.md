# Where distinctive alternatives come from

An alternative is good when it could only belong to this product. Test every suggestion: would it fit a competitor just as well? If yes, find another.

## 1. The product's own truth

The thing only this product does, shows or knows. Lead with the real interface moment that delivers value (the approval, the saving, the answer), not a generic device mockup.

## 2. Real content and data

Actual numbers with sources, real customer language from research or reviews, real examples. A specific figure with a footnote beats a round number every time.

## 3. Domain material

The visual and verbal material of the domain the user lives in: the ledger, the meter reading, the timetable, the case file, the receipt. Borrow its structure, typography or rhythm.

## 4. Brand assets that already exist

Logo geometry, a proprietary typeface, a colour with history, photography style, a tone of voice document. Push these further before adding anything new.

## 5. Typographic voice

A considered type pairing, a distinctive scale, editorial hierarchy, real numerals for data. Type is the cheapest way to sound like someone.

## 6. Editorial structure

Arrange the page around the user's actual questions in the order they ask them, not the template sequence. Let sections differ in rhythm and density when their content differs.

## 7. Motion that explains

Animate state changes, cause and effect, or progress. Remove motion that only decorates.

## 8. Copy with a point of view

Say the specific, slightly uncomfortable true thing. Replace benefit claims with evidence. Write CTAs that describe what happens next ("See your rate in 2 minutes", "Check if you're eligible").
