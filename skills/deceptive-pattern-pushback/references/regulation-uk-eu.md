# Regulatory context: UK and EU

Orientation only, not legal advice. Several provisions are being phased in or amended. **Before citing any item in a brief, check its current status** (search the regulator's site if web access is available) and recommend legal or compliance review where the risk is real. Mark anything unverified as "check current status" in the output.

## United Kingdom

| Regime | Who it covers | Relevant to | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (unfair commercial practices) | All traders dealing with consumers | Misleading actions and omissions, aggressive practices, drip pricing, fake reviews | Replaced the CPRs 2008 from April 2025. The CMA can enforce directly and impose significant fines. Includes a list of banned practices. |
| DMCC Act 2024 (subscription contracts) | Subscription businesses | Sign-up information, reminders, easy cancellation, cooling-off | Commencement has been phased later than the rest of the Act. Check current status before citing. |
| FCA Consumer Duty | FCA-regulated firms | Consumer understanding, price and value, consumer support, avoiding foreseeable harm | Sludge (making it harder to cancel, switch or complain than to buy) is a specific concern. Particularly relevant to banking, lending, insurance, payments. |
| UK GDPR and PECR | Anyone processing personal data or using cookies and marketing | Consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous; pre-ticked boxes are not valid consent | The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amends parts of this, including some cookie rules. Check current status. |
| ICO and CMA joint position on harmful design | Guidance | Harmful online choice architecture in privacy and consumer choices | Useful as a citation for how both regulators view these patterns. |
| Ofcom General Conditions | Telecoms providers | Contract information, price rises, switching and exit | Contract price rises must be set out in pounds and pence at the point of sale. |
| CAP Code (ASA) | Advertisers | Misleading advertising, urgency, pricing claims | Applies to marketing pages and ads. |

## European Union

| Regime | Who it covers | Relevant to | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unfair Commercial Practices Directive | Traders dealing with consumers | Misleading and aggressive practices | Commission guidance covers dark patterns explicitly. |
| Digital Services Act, Article 25 | Online platforms | Interfaces that deceive, manipulate or impair free choice | Applies to platforms, not every website. |
| GDPR and ePrivacy | Anyone processing personal data | Consent design, cookie banners | Reject must be as easy as accept, per multiple regulator decisions. |
| Consumer Rights Directive, including the withdrawal function amendments | Distance contracts | Pre-contract information, withdrawal | A withdrawal button requirement for online contracts is being introduced. Check current status. |
| AI Act, Article 5 | AI systems on the EU market | Prohibits manipulative or deceptive techniques that materially distort behaviour and cause significant harm | Relevant to assistants and agents. |
| Digital Fairness Act | Proposed | Dark patterns, addictive design, subscription traps | At proposal stage. Check current status. |

## How to use this in a brief

- Cite the regime, not the clause, unless you have verified the clause.
- Pair each regulatory point with the user harm it protects against; that is the stronger argument internally.
- For regulated sectors (financial services, telecoms, energy), the sector regulator usually matters more than general consumer law. Lead with it.
